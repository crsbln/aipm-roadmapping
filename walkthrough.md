# Walkthrough for the Sponsor Review

A spoken walkthrough of about ten minutes, presented directly from the artifacts. No slide deck. The visual anchor
is the roadmap table in `roadmap.md`. Commitment words are chosen on purpose and must not be strengthened when this
is retold. Status: drafted by Claude, reviewed and accepted by the owner on 2026-11-06 (A13 records one correction).

## 1. Vision, strategy, objectives

- **Vision:** creators decide to launch, to change their plan, or to wait, with an honest picture of their odds
  instead of a hope.
- **Strategy:** Kickstarter creators, before launch, plan-level inputs only. Value comes from a calibrated chance
  relative to the 36 % average and from three evidence-backed patterns shown as patterns, never as a recipe.
  We will not build an optimizer, promise success, cover other platforms, or sell anything on the current data.
- **Objectives:** O1 creators make informed launch decisions. O2 the estimate stays trustworthy on today's campaigns.
  O3 Launch Check can be used legitimately and run sustainably.

## 2. The strongest evidence and the most important limitations

- **Strongest:** 372,293 finished campaigns. Goal, category and campaign length explain a meaningful part of the
  outcome: up to 1,000 USD goals are funded 49 % of the time, over 100,000 USD 6 %; Dance 62 % vs Technology 20 %;
  the 30-day default (45 % of all campaigns) is funded 32 % vs 46 % one day shorter. The model ranks better than a
  rule of thumb (0.754 vs 0.637) and its percentages mean what they say on average (35 % → 35 %).
- **Limitations:** the data ends in January 2018 and is licensed non-commercial. The model cannot see the campaign
  itself: video, rewards, audience. And we have **not spoken to a single creator**. Everything about what creators
  want is an assumption.

## 3. Format and horizons

Now-Next-Later as **confidence horizons**, not calendar columns. Now means we are actively learning, with a named
question and a stop signal. Next means we intend to, once a named gate opens. Later means we are watching an option.
Items move on evidence or on a recorded decision, never with the passing of time. There are no dates because nothing
has a genuine date.

## 4. Selected initiatives and explicit exclusions

- **Now:** learn from creators (I01); establish a lawful and current data basis (I02).
- **Next:** honest presentation of the estimate (I04, waits for creators to test with); trustworthy estimate on
  current campaigns (I03, blocked until the data basis exists).
- **Later:** deliberate choices on goal and length (I05); seeing beyond the plan (I06, paused).
- **Excluded:** a what-if optimizer, success promises, other platforms, commercial use of the current dataset,
  predicting pledged amounts, a paid tier or app before we have heard creators, any deployment before the cost
  check, generated campaign advice, and a paid launch or launch date in the next phase.

## 5. Confidence, dependencies, gates, commitment levels

- **Exploring:** I01, I02. **Intending:** I03, I04. **Options:** I05, I06. **Committed:** nothing. **Forecast:**
  nothing. **Fixed:** the licence, the privacy rule and the cost gate.
- **Gates:** legal (licence and platform terms) on I02, I03, I06; privacy on I01, I02, I06; cost before any
  deployment; accessibility for any public page.
- **Dependencies:** I04 and I05 need I01; I03 and I06 need I02.

## 6. The change we introduced and how the roadmap responded

- The dataset licence was confirmed as CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: non-commercial. In the scenario, the sponsor asked for a
  paid service by the spring season.
- We did not add it. There is no lawful data basis, no demand evidence, and no genuine date. We moved the data-basis
  question to the front, extended it with "decide the intended use together with the sponsor", paused the
  campaign-content option, and wrote the paid launch into the exclusions with the conditions under which it could
  return.
- Nothing that was previously promised changed, because nothing was promised.

## 7. What we review next

- When the first creator conversations are analysed (T1): is this worth building, free or paid, and does the honest
  presentation enter Now?
- When the legal reading and an alternative data source are assessed (T2): does retraining unblock, and can a paid
  offering ever be discussed?
- Whichever comes first opens review R2.

**Closing line:** "What you are looking at is a learning plan with two open gates, not a delivery plan. We intend to
retrain the model once a lawful data basis exists, and we intend to show creators an honest number once we know how
they read it. Neither is a date."
